<br><br>Just a few weeks after joining MoonMuse Entertainment LLC, I took part in Global Game Jam 2025 with the team.  The theme was Bubbles, and we also took on a few of the jam's optional diversifiers, including a Y2K look.
<br><br>The result is AquaPop 2000, a 3D platformer made in Unity.  Chloe has lost all of her flip phones, and you help her find them in AquaPop before her friends think she ghosted them, while the friendly fish of the region try to distract her.  It has a browser version and is best played with a controller.
<br><br>I was one of two programmers on a team of eight, and those fish were my job.  I built the swordfish, puffer fish, sea urchin, sawshark, and stingray, each with its own behavior.  The swordfish, for example, notices you, swims after you, and heads back home when it loses interest, while the puffer fish watches with a cone of vision.  They all roam on Unity's NavMesh, and I added the health and damage they share with the player.  Unfortunately, because of the jam's time constraints, most of the enemies didn't make it into the game, and the focus shifted to platforming.  However, I kind of used the sawshark as a testbed for how we may want to have flying enemies move in IVOR.
<br><br>I also built the menus: the main menu, pause menu, and a settings menu covering resolution, fullscreen, and the retro PlayStation-style shader, all usable with a mouse or a controller.  The drop shadow under Chloe and the fish, which makes jumps much easier to judge, was mine too.  It has to be placed manually, because the PSX style has no lighting or shadows.
<br><br>After the jam I kept working on it for a couple of weeks.  I got the web and Linux builds working, moved the game to Unity's Universal Render Pipeline (URP), reworked the title cards and music that play as you move between areas, and gave the menu another pass.
